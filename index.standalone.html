01

Walk-forward factor backtest

Does a machine-learned equity signal survive real costs and honest scoring?

Open repo

1.34out-of-sample Sharpe, the linear baseline. Random forest and gradient boosting both lost to it on a 24-ticker panel once friction is priced in.

AimFind out whether a learned equity signal survives real costs and honest out-of-sample scoring, not just a good-looking in-sample fit.
How I did itRanked 24 US mega-caps by predicted one-month return, long the top 8, quarterly rebalance at 10 bps. Trained walk-forward, on data before each target year only, with a FRED macro overlay, and priced every trade.
ResourcesPython, scikit-learn, LightGBM, pandas, yfinance, FRED. 24-ticker panel, 2017-2024. 16 tests. About 1.5 weeks.
FindingsOOS Sharpe 1.34 for the linear baseline; random forest and gradient boosting both lost to it. A sector-neutral cap lifted LightGBM IR to 0.56.
Why it mattersThis is the thesis of the whole portfolio. With a small cross-section, model depth is a liability, and the walk-forward split is the tool that exposes it.
Skill learnedWalk-forward evaluation and cost-aware backtesting. Reading a loss as a result, not a bug to delete.
02

A VaR desk that admits its errors

Can a one-day 99% Value-at-Risk actually hold up against ten years of daily equity data?

Open repo

0.994SPY GARCH persistence. The 99% VaR recorded 7 violations in 552 days (1.27% against a 1% target), and the Kupiec test failed to reject correct coverage at p = 0.53.

AimBuild a Value-at-Risk desk whose coverage can be tested against a decade of daily returns, not just asserted on paper.
How I did itGARCH(1,1)-t and EGARCH volatility forecasting on SPY, Kupiec and Christoffersen coverage backtests on the 99% VaR, then a 15% vol-target overlay against a static 50/50 benchmark.
Resourcesarch, scipy, pandas, FRED risk-free series. 2015-2026 daily equity data. 9 tests. About 3 weeks.
FindingsGARCH persistence 0.994; 7 of 552 VaR violations against a 1% target, Kupiec p = 0.53. The vol-target cut max drawdown to 11.4% but traded Sharpe for it.
Why it mattersIt closes the "have you modeled market data?" gap every target school probes, and it is the natural next layer for the capstone's regime signal.
Skill learnedGARCH MLE and VaR coverage backtesting. Telling a risk tool from a return amplifier, out loud.

Earnings calls, read as data

Does the language of an earnings release move the stock after the print?

Open repo

0.055abnormal return per dollar of earnings surprise, significant at the 5% level across 160 events. Tone, on its own, did not reach significance, and the paper says why.

AimTest whether the language of an earnings release moves the stock after the print, using a defensible event-study design rather than a vibes check.
How I did it160 firm-quarter events across 10 firms: SEC EDGAR release text, deterministic tone and uncertainty scoring, market-model abnormal returns, then a Newey-West HAC regression with firm fixed effects.
ResourcesSEC EDGAR, statsmodels HAC inference, yfinance. 160 events, 10 firms. 5 tests. About 3 weeks, run in parallel with project 3.
FindingsEarnings surprise is the significant driver of post-announcement drift; lexicon sentiment and uncertainty do not reach significance at this sample size, and the paper says so.
Why it mattersThe ML-by-finance differentiator on this list. Stating the limit of a word-list scorer honestly is itself the finding, and the LLM upgrade is the documented next step.
Skill learnedEvent-study design and HAC inference. Knowing where a cheap scorer stops being enough.

How the work is made

Nothing sees the future

Every split is walk-forward or strictly lagged. The label a model trains on is only ever a return that had not happened yet at prediction time, and the backtest engine prices the trade in the same period the decision is made. If a project leaks, it would fail its own tests first.

Losses stay in the writeup

Where a model did not beat the benchmark, that is the reported result: the linear baseline outrunning the ensembles, the tone overlay underperforming, the volatility target trading Sharpe for drawdowns. Recruiters test whether a candidate can hold a negative result without deleting it.

Reproducible by one command

Each pipeline is cache-first, so a full re-run costs nothing and needs no network. Fifty test cases across the six repositories check the accounting, the splits, and the statistics before any number is quoted here.

Designed, then seconded

The designs, the splits, the cost models, and every number above are mine, and they are the parts an interviewer will press on. A large language model assisted with boilerplate and literature, the way one would with a strong intern, and the result was reviewed line by line before it went public.
