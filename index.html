A VaR desk that admits its errors

Can a one-day 99% Value-at-Risk actually hold up against ten years of daily equity data?

0.994SPY GARCH persistence. The 99% VaR recorded 7 violations in 552 days (1.27% against a 1% target), and the Kupiec test failed to reject correct coverage at p = 0.53.

Use caseRisk budgeting: GARCH(1,1)-t and EGARCH vol forecasting, coverage backtests, and a 15% vol-target overlay tested against a static 50/50.
Stackarch, scipy, pandas, FRED risk-free series
Effort3 weeks. 9 tests.
LearnedVol-targeting cuts drawdowns but trades Sharpe for them. The overlay is a return amplifier, and the desk note says so instead of pretending otherwise.

How the work is made

Nothing sees the future

Every split is walk-forward or strictly lagged. The label a model trains on is only ever a return that had not happened yet at prediction time, and the backtest engine prices the trade in the same period the decision is made. If a project leaks, it would fail its own tests first.

Losses stay in the writeup

Where a model did not beat the benchmark, that is the reported result: the linear baseline outrunning the ensembles, the tone overlay underperforming, the volatility target trading Sharpe for drawdowns. Recruiters test whether a candidate can hold a negative result without deleting it.

Reproducible by one command

Each pipeline is cache-first, so a full re-run costs nothing and needs no network. Fifty test cases across the six repositories check the accounting, the splits, and the statistics before any number is quoted here.

Designed, then seconded

The designs, the splits, the cost models, and every number above are mine, and they are the parts an interviewer will press on. A large language model assisted with boilerplate and literature, the way one would with a strong intern, and the result was reviewed line by line before it went public.
